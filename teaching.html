GEOL3140 Hydrogeology

Where does our drinking water come from, how does contamination move underground, and why do rivers, wetlands, and coastlines depend on groundwater? Hydrogeology gives students the tools to answer these questions and prepares them for careers in water resources, environmental consulting, field science, and graduate study.

  • Explore hidden water systems: Students learn how aquifers store water, how groundwater flows, and how wells are used to understand the subsurface.
  • Work with real hydrogeology data: Students use slug-test, well, and groundwater-flow data to estimate aquifer properties and solve practical water problems.
  • Connect science to global water challenges: Student groups research a major water issue and explain why it matters for people and the environment.
  • See hydrogeology in action: Field examples from local teaching wells and coastal aquifers show how classroom concepts are used outside the university.

ENVI3010 GIS in Earth and Environmental Sciences

GIS is one of the most useful skills in environmental science, geology, planning, public health, and climate work. In this hands-on course, students learn ArcGIS Pro by making maps, analyzing spatial data, collecting GPS information, and turning environmental questions into clear visual stories.

  • Build job-ready mapping skills: Students use ArcGIS Pro to work with maps, coordinate systems, raster data, attribute tables, queries, terrain analysis, and ModelBuilder.
  • Collect data outdoors: Students use GPS and mobile mapping tools to gather field data and bring it into a GIS project.
  • Use the GIS Sand Table: Students explore how terrain controls watersheds, flow paths, and landscape patterns.
  • Map the campus tree inventory: Students turn field-collected tree data into professional maps with clear legends, layouts, and takeaways.
  • Create a final Story Map: Students choose a real-world question, find data, run GIS analysis, and present results in an ArcGIS Story Map portfolio piece.

GEOL5250 Groundwater Modeling

Groundwater models help communities plan water supplies, evaluate contamination risks, and test what may happen before decisions are made in the real world. This graduate course teaches students how to build, run, and explain groundwater models used in research, consulting, and water management.

  • Learn the tools behind water decisions: Students use finite-difference methods, MODFLOW, and solute transport models to study groundwater flow and contaminant movement.
  • Solve realistic groundwater scenarios: Projects such as Aquiferia, Remson Gulch, and Kryptonite ask students to build models, test ideas, and interpret results.
  • Read models like a professional: Students critique a peer-reviewed MODFLOW study and learn how to judge whether a model is useful and trustworthy.
  • Build a modeling portfolio piece: A larger course project gives students experience explaining model results, uncertainty, and real-world meaning.

GEOL4260/5260 Lab and Field Methods in Hydrogeology

This course gets students outside, into the lab, and working with the tools hydrogeologists use in the field. Students learn how to collect reliable data, keep professional field notes, measure water movement, and turn observations into useful interpretations.

  • Train like a field scientist: Students practice field sketching, field notebooks, site descriptions, equipment records, and clear data documentation.
  • Measure a real stream: Students conduct stream gaging at Stony Brook and learn how surface water and groundwater are connected.
  • Survey water levels accurately: Students use differential leveling and water-level measurements to compare hydraulic heads across field sites.
  • Test aquifer behavior: Students use slug tests, pump tests, and hydraulic-head data to estimate how easily water moves underground.
  • Run hands-on lab experiments: Darcy column and Ogata-Banks experiments let students measure flow, track solute movement, and interpret breakthrough curves.
